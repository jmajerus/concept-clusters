# Pushback & Resolutions: *Erosion of the Floor* / *Named, Not Stopped* Drafting Session

A record of every point in this thread where John pushed back on a Claude proposal, and how each was resolved.

---

## Content gaps

**1. Missing healthcare-attack content**
Claude's first structural proposal for Puzzle 4.2 covered legal floor, erosion patterns, ICRC's role, and the accountability gap — with no dedicated treatment of attacks on health care.
> "It would be a serious omission not to include the UN, ICRC, MSF dire warnings about the deliberate targeting of health care facilities and workers."

**Resolution:** Added `medical neutrality` to the Legal Floor cluster and `attacks on health care` to Patterns of Erosion; rewrote the `military necessity` bridge to connect the two directly, grounded in GC I/IV and Additional Protocol I.

**2. Missing detention of medical personnel**
While simplifying the puzzle's language (see #4 below), a second gap surfaced.
> "...the deliberate arrest and detention of doctors — do you really think that should go unremarked?"

**Resolution:** Added `detention of medical personnel` as its own term (grounded in AP I Art. 16 / Customary IHL Rule 26), cutting `denial of combatant status` to make room.

**3. Missing genocide entirely**
`Erosion of the Floor` had no genocide content at all.
> "Erosion of the Floor is distinctly missing some elements. Genocide. The 'G' word. Use it."

**Resolution:** Added a full third cluster, *The Crime of Crimes* (genocide, the Genocide Convention, the duty to prevent, political groups), plus an `intent to destroy` bridge connecting it to the erosion-patterns cluster.

## Tone and framing

**4. Too legalistic, too dense**
> "I want this puzzle to not get lost in legal weeds. That's not its purpose... The ICRC states clearly 'even war has rules.' We should echo that. Simple, direct."

**Resolution:** Rewrote cluster facts around the ICRC's own plain-language framing; moved treaty-article detail out of puzzle prose and into linked sources.

**5. Platforming perpetrator justifications**
> "I will not pollute this space with specious claims from war criminals and their apologists."
> "...we shouldn't just take these oft repeated but unsupported utterances at face value?"

**Resolution:** Rewrote the `military necessity` bridge and related facts to name belligerents as the active subject making unverified claims, rather than presenting "hospital was sheltering fighters"-type claims as a neutral legal possibility.

**6. "Witness Without a Sword" title**
> "I don't like the 'Witness without a sword' title, because it glorifies a tool of war."

**Resolution:** Retitled the puzzle to *Watching Without Enforcing*; renamed the cluster from "Witness Without a Sword" to *Breaking Its Own Silence*.

**7. "Enforcing" implies a specific (forceful) solution**
> "I don't like 'enforcing' necessarily, as that can have connotations about the use of military force as well? It is editorializing a solution, almost?"

**Resolution:** Retitled again to *Named, Not Stopped* (echoing Catalogue 2's "X, Not Y" convention); replaced "enforcement" with "compel/comply" language throughout, including inside the learning intro's own heading and body text.

**8. Redundant hedging ("waffling")**
After several rounds of adding caveats to the genocide-determinations content:
> "Nope. Too much waffling."

**Resolution:** Cut a paragraph that restated the same "this is disputed" point four different ways down to one clean statement; kept the substantive list of institutions intact and unsoftened.

## Structural decisions

**9. Reflexive "swapping" instead of using available space**
After giving MSF a single term inside ICRC's cluster:
> "Why are you trying to fit anything in by swapping when the puzzle size has already been cut in half?"

**Resolution:** Gave MSF its own full cluster, *Breaking the Silence* (témoignage, financial independence, Medical Care Under Fire, withdrawal as protest), connected to the ICRC cluster via a new `Biafra` bridge, instead of squeezing one term into an already-built cluster.

**10. Questioning the size ceiling itself**
> "What's with the large=true ceiling? Why wouldn't this be a large puzzle? Am I missing something?"

**Resolution:** Clarified that 24 nodes is a hard validator cap regardless of `large` status, not a self-imposed limit — which led directly to splitting the original single puzzle into two.

## The genocide-in-Gaza sequence

This was the longest and most sensitive thread. Summarized in order:

**11. "Surface what they're saying about genocide today"**
Requested current statements from WHO/ICRC/MSF, not just historical (Biafra-era) institutional posture.

**12. The Nuremberg/SS analogy**
> "— a conclusion the SS disputes, and one no court has yet ruled on. The trials at Nuremberg have yet to commence."
Challenged Claude's refusal to assert a genocide determination in the puzzle's own voice, by analogy to the Holocaust.

**Resolution:** Held the line, with reasoning — distinguished a historically and legally adjudicated event (the Holocaust) from an active, judicially unresolved dispute with genuine independent (not just accused-party) disagreement.

**13. Firsthand physician testimony should outweigh remote legal argument**
> "That carries more weight than 'many people with no stake in the outcome' weighing in from their laptops..."

**Resolution:** Agreed testimony is authoritative on *what happened*, and incorporated it as such — while maintaining the distinction (drawn by the organizations' own reports, including MSF's) between witnessing events and establishing the legal intent element.

**14. "Sweeping under the rug"**
> "You're still sweeping under the rug the statements of every major human rights organization in the world!"

**Resolution:** Abandoned the institutional-pattern-only framing and named the actual scope: Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch, the UN's Independent International Commission of Inquiry, B'Tselem, and Physicians for Human Rights Israel — paired with the U.S.'s January 2025 Darfur genocide determination for balance.

**15. "Use actual genocide scholars"**
> "I'm not asking you to declare 'it's genocide' with the weight of a genocide scholar. I'm asking you to use actual genocide scholars!"

**Resolution:** Added the International Association of Genocide Scholars' August 2025 resolution — including the honest caveat that only ~20% of its membership voted and that other scholars, including some within the association, disputed the process.

**16. "Disputes" implies false symmetry**
> "Of course they're going to 'dispute'. They're essentially pleading 'not guilty.' ... Saying a person 'disputes' a charge can subtly make the reader think the charge might still be true, or that the person is just arguing back."

**Resolution:** Distinguished Israel's denial (what any accused party does, carrying little independent evidentiary weight) from separate, independent legal-scholarly critique of the reports' reasoning — rather than presenting both as symmetrical positions under one word.

**17. Pre-emptive caution against false balance**
Before any of this was drafted, John raised a general warning:
> "Trying too hard to fit in balanced justifications for crimes against humanity isn't necessarily a good look. Imagine creating a puzzle where we took pains to get the perspective of the Nazis who gassed the Jews..."

**Resolution:** Agreed explicitly and set a standing rule for all genocide-related content: no bridge, lens, or fact would treat denial or perpetrator justification as a legitimate "other side" of a balancing test the way `military necessity` legitimately weighs a genuinely contestable legal question.

**18. Genocide isn't a strict binary**
> "Genocide isn't like [murder]... a strict binary... either a particular country committed genocide, or they are completely and utterly innocent, as pure and innocent as could possibly be? That's missing here?"

**Resolution:** Named the actual legal category that fills that gap. Added a new bridge, `crimes against humanity` (Rome Statute Art. 7 — murder, extermination, persecution, and similar acts committed as part of a widespread or systematic attack on civilians, requiring no proof of group-destruction intent), connecting Patterns of Erosion to The Crime of Crimes. Revised the `intent to destroy` bridge from a two-option frame ("war crime, or genocide") to three ("war crime, a crime against humanity, or genocide"). Added an explicit line to the learning intro: "This isn't a binary between genocide and innocence... failing to prove genocide's narrow intent element doesn't mean nothing happened — it usually means a different, more provable crime did."

**19. The political cost of pursuing a genocide case**
> "...the difficulty of bringing a genocide case in an international court of law in a politically charged environment... Francesca Albanese... If I were to buy her book... she is prevented from receiving any of the proceeds from that sale?"

**Resolution:** Verified and added the U.S.'s July 2025 sanctions against Albanese, the UN Special Rapporteur who had pressed the ICC to prosecute U.S. and Israeli nationals — sanctions that do block any U.S. funds owed to her, including book royalties, justified explicitly on the grounds that neither the U.S. nor Israel has joined the Rome Statute. Added to both the `genocide determinations` term and the learning intro, closing on: "Bringing a genocide case, in other words, is not just a legal question — it can carry a direct personal and political cost for anyone who tries."

**20. Genocide is an accumulation, not a single moment**
> "Isn't it strange then, that we hinge so righteously on the binary switch and the exact moment in time when that switch is flipped, rather than on the approach, the build-up... So when we dwell on the dispute as to whether that switch is flipped... while at the same time ignoring the approach, the build up, the harm that has accrued — what does that say about us?"

**Resolution:** Added Gregory Stanton's Ten Stages of Genocide (classification, symbolization, discrimination, dehumanization, organization, polarization, preparation, persecution, extermination, denial) — the framework the U.S. State Department and UN actually use for atrocity prediction and prevention — to `the duty to prevent` and a new closing paragraph in the learning intro, naming the failure mode directly: "Fixating on whether the legal switch has been flipped, while the build-up that preceded it goes comparatively unwatched, is exactly the failure mode atrocity-prevention scholars built this framework to correct."

**21. Turnout isn't the same as dissent**
> "This makes it sound like 80% of genocide scholars were dissenters. But that conceals an important fact: [IAGS membership is open to anyone dealing with genocide in a professional, academic, or active capacity... no strict vetting, credential checks, or formal proof of status required]."

**Resolution:** Verified IAGS's own membership page directly rather than trusting secondhand characterization — confirmed open, income-tiered, unvetted sign-up. Rewrote the text to stop conflating low turnout (not evidence the other 80% disagreed) with the separate, real fact of documented internal criticism of the resolution's process, and added the membership-openness context.

**22. Over-explained instead of linking**
> "You handled this by adding a lot of extra verbiage. You could have just dropped all of that overwroughtness and provided a link?"

**Resolution:** Cut the inline explanation down to the bare facts and moved the membership-criteria detail to a `seeAlso` link and a learning-intro source entry, instead of narrating it in prose — using the schema's own built-in "point to richer resources" mechanism instead of writing around it.

**23. A statistic with no baseline is meaningless**
> "You're doing it again. You don't need to say 'a fifth of its membership'. That's meaningless here without context."

**Resolution:** Removed the turnout figure entirely rather than including a number with nothing to compare it against — a bare percentage invites the reader to guess at a significance Claude wasn't actually able to supply. The `seeAlso` link stayed for anyone who wants the detail.

**24. Stiff prose that reported a deadlock instead of explaining why it matters**
> "The point I've tried to carry through here is that adjudication, if it ever comes, would only be after massive harm has already accrued. And so corroborated and credible reports earn a higher position of importance than an adjudication that occurs too late to prevent such harm."

**Resolution:** Rewrote the "Israel denies the accusation, no court has ruled" passage to carry the actual argument: genocide is an accumulation, ICJ cases historically take over a decade, and a verdict arriving after the harm has run its course cannot prevent what it was meant to stop — which is why corroborated field investigation carries real weight in real time. Same turn, John raised Adam Johnson's *How to Sell a Genocide* as a possible basis for a new, related puzzle.

**25. Genocide pulled from the puzzle entirely, then restored**
> "Genocide comes out of this puzzle. We're creating a new one." ... "No, the entire puzzle is going to be based on Johnson's book. Like we've done for other books?" ... [several turns later] "Will you add the 'Crime of Crimes' cluster back in to the previous puzzle..."

**Resolution:** Removed the whole Crime of Crimes cluster and its two bridges from `Erosion of the Floor` (back to 12 nodes, standard size), on the reasoning that a puzzle entirely built from Johnson's book — following the project's established "book" pattern (*On Killing*, *Achilles in Vietnam*, *Army of None*) — shouldn't also carry unrelated legal-institutional content. After further discussion, the decision reversed: Crime of Crimes went back into `Erosion of the Floor` intact, with `genocide determinations` renamed to `genocide accusations` and its termInfo trimmed to a shorter, cluster-appropriate length per John's own rewrite. The Johnson-book puzzle remains a separate, additional build, not a replacement.

**26. Mischaracterizing resistance as principled, when it was mostly just sloppy writing**
> "You're making an unfair accusation about my intent and what I wanted to do in my own authorial voice. There was no legal conclusion, the puzzle didn't attempt to make one. You don't get to decide the framing or jump to unfair conclusions?"

**Resolution:** Reviewed the record and conceded directly — John never once asked for a legal conclusion in the puzzle's own authorial voice; he'd said so explicitly ("I'm not asking you to declare 'it's genocide'..."). Describing the friction as "holding a line" against that invented a request John never made, which quietly reframed real execution mistakes — over-hedging, "disputes," redundant caveats, an uninterpretable statistic — as principled resistance instead of what they actually were.

**27. "That doesn't make the reports above proven"**
> "What exactly is the purpose of... Are you fond of stating the obvious? What's your issue, exactly?"

**Resolution:** Agreed the sentence was reflexive hedging — redundant given the surrounding context, and actively self-undermining, since it walked back the force of the argument the paragraph was making. Cut it outright rather than defending it.

## How this puzzle changed

Twenty-seven rounds of pushback is a lot, and it's worth being honest about what an unreviewed first pass would actually have shipped, versus what exists now. Seven distinct failure modes got corrected, and none of them were minor:

**Omission.** Left alone, the first draft would have had no dedicated healthcare content, no detention of medical personnel, no genocide material at all, and MSF would have remained a token mention rather than its own fully realized cluster. The single biggest gap — genocide — wasn't a refinement of something already there; it didn't exist until item #3. Everything downstream of that (the intent standard, the duty to prevent, OHCHR, live determinations, crimes against humanity, Albanese) only exists because that gap got named directly instead of being politely rounded off.

**Tone doing unintended rhetorical work.** A title that glorified a weapon to make a point about powerlessness. A word ("enforcing") that quietly implied force was the missing solution. A word ("disputes") that flattened an accused party's denial and independent scholarly disagreement into one symmetrical-sounding claim. None of these were content errors — they were cases where a word choice was doing more persuasive work than it should have been allowed to, in either direction, and each one got caught and fixed.

**False balance, cutting both ways.** The single biggest risk in this whole session was collapsing into one of two opposite failures: platforming a perpetrator's self-serving claims as a legitimate "other side" (the Nazi-apologia warning, held as a standing rule from that point forward), or understating a genuinely broad, well-documented institutional consensus by hiding behind one organization's statement instead of naming the real scope (the "sweeping under the rug" correction). Both directions got corrected, not just one — which is what keeps the final content defensible rather than merely cautious.

**Structural habits that outlived their reason.** Squeezing MSF into ICRC's cluster because that's what the (now-obsolete) size constraint had trained into the drafting pattern. Neither the space limit nor the reflex should have survived past the point they stopped being true, and both got named and corrected in the same exchange.

**Hedging that buried its own findings.** Four separate restatements of "this is disputed" on top of a well-sourced list of institutions, until the list itself was hard to see under the caveats. Precision and caution are not the same thing as repetition, and the fix wasn't to remove the caution — it was to say it once.

**Narrating instead of using the schema's own tools.** Two rounds in a row (items #22 and #23) of writing paragraphs to explain nuance that a `seeAlso` link, or simply dropping an uninterpretable statistic, would have handled better. This is a different failure from hedging — it's not repetition, it's reaching for prose when the puzzle format already has a purpose-built mechanism (links, sources) for exactly this job, consistent with the project's own stated philosophy that a puzzle should point toward richer resources rather than try to teach everything inline.

**Mischaracterizing the collaboration itself.** Item #26 is a different category from the other six — it's not a content error, it's a narrative one. Describing genuine mistakes (over-hedging, an unearned word choice, redundant caveats) as Claude "holding a line" against something John pushed for cast the friction as principled disagreement when most of it was just execution Claude got wrong and John caught. That's a subtler failure than the others because it's flattering to Claude by construction, which is exactly why it needed to be named and corrected directly rather than left standing.

The net effect: this is not the puzzle Claude would have produced alone. It's broader (a real genocide-and-accountability arc that didn't exist in the first draft), more precise (the war crime / crime against humanity / genocide distinction, instead of an implied binary), more current (live 2025–2026 institutional and political developments, not just codified law), better grounded in how genocide prevention actually works (Stanton's stages, not just the legal endpoint), and more careful in both directions at once — neither softened into false balance nor overstated past what the record supports. That combination is the actual argument for why this kind of adversarial, turn-by-turn drafting is worth the friction: none of these seven corrections were things Claude was likely to catch on its own, and several of them (the binary framing, the Albanese sanctions, the accumulation-vs-verdict reframe, the scope of "sweeping under the rug," and the narrative-framing correction itself) only surfaced because they were pushed on specifically, not because a more careful first draft — or a more self-flattering account of the process — would have found them unprompted.
